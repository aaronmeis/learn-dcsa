2. TOGAF ADM Phase A & B / DoDAF Operational Viewpoints (OV-1, OV-5b)

2.1 High-Level Operational Concept & Vision (TOGAF Phase A / DoDAF OV-1 — Conceptual Level)

Organizational Context & Agency Integration History

To understand the technical topology of the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) operating model, enterprise architects must ground system realizations in the agency's structural evolution. Codified as the primary security enterprise for the Department of Defense (DoD) and Executive Branch partners, DCSA was established on October 1, 2019, through the strategic consolidation of three legacy entities: 1. The Office of Personnel Management’s (OPM) National Background Investigation Bureau (NBIB). 2. The DoD Defense Security Service (DSS). 3. The DoD Consolidated Adjudications Facility (DoD CAF).

This initial merger was expanded on October 1, 2020, through the transfer of four legacy IT infrastructure and security capabilities: background investigation IT systems from OPM/DISA, the National Background Investigation System (NBIS) program executive office, background/security IT systems from the Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC), and the National Center for Credibility Assessment (NCCA) from the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA). In FY22, DCSA absorbed the DIA Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) accreditation mission.

This multi-stage consolidation explains why physical architecture models contain distinct systems of record (e.g., NISS, NBIS, DMDC legacy databases, DISS) that are actively undergoing transition, requiring rigorous verification of system names.

Problem Statement

Cleared industry must protect classified national security information while remaining commercially competitive. Under the National Industrial Security Program (NISP), codified at 32 CFR Part 117, DCSA serves as the government authority administering facility and personnel security eligibility.

The foundational architectural principle is dual eligibility: a Facility Clearance (FCL) and a Personnel Clearance (PCL) lock together sequentially and operationally. Neither track alone provides access to classified material. Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) represents an unmitigated risk gate that can halt, block, or revoke facility eligibility regardless of technical or contractual capability.

Four Primary Mission Domains

  1. Industrial Security / NISP: Facility security eligibility is granted, maintained, and overseen pursuant to 32 CFR Part 117. * Governance Stack: 32 CFR Part 117 $\rightarrow$ DCSA Handbooks $\rightarrow$ CDSE FSO Curricula $\rightarrow$ Facility Practice.
  2. Personnel Vetting: Individuals are background-investigated, adjudicated under national criteria, and enrolled in continuous vetting. * Governance Stack: 32 CFR Part 117 $\rightarrow$ SEAD 4 Guidelines $\rightarrow$ CDSE PCL Courses $\rightarrow$ Adjudicative Practice.
  3. Information System (IS) Authorization: Contractor classified systems processing, storing, or transmitting classified data are accredited under the Risk Management Framework (RMF) overlaid by the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAPM v2.2) (historical; now the DAAG). * Governance Stack: DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) Manual $\rightarrow$ NIST SP 800-37 RMF $\rightarrow$ CDSE A&A Courses $\rightarrow$ System Package Practice.
  4. Counterintelligence (CI): Industry maintains an active insider-threat detection, reporting, and counterintelligence culture to defeat adversarial collection efforts. * Governance Stack: DoD CI Directives $\rightarrow$ CDSE FSO Toolkit $\rightarrow$ Escalation Drills.

Twelve Architectural Actors & Trust Boundaries

  • Government Contracting Activity (GCA): USG mission owner requiring classified contract performance. Holds high USG authority; initiates sponsorship based on valid contractual need.
  • Cleared Prime Contractor: Industrial facility holding an active FCL with a classified contract requiring subcontractor performance. Holds high NISP trust; bound by contract to sponsor qualified subcontractors.
  • Uncleared Applicant Company: Legal commercial or academic entity seeking entry into the NISP. Positioned in an untrusted boundary until sponsored, vetted, and granted facility eligibility.
  • Facility Security Officer (FSO): Corporate security lead responsible for facility package assembly, NISP compliance, and adverse information reporting. Holds trusted internal security oversight.
  • Information System Security Manager (ISSM): Corporate technical lead responsible for classified system authorization packages under DAAG/RMF. Holds trusted system administration authority.
  • Key Management Personnel (KMP): Executive officers, board members, partners, and managers exercising authority over corporate policy or contract execution. Architectural Rule: KMPs are evaluated strictly on security-affecting authority and operational control, not corporate titles alone.
  • Industrial Security Representative (ISR): DCSA field official providing government oversight, facility reviews, and security self-inspection audits. Holds high USG trust.
  • Adjudicators: USG personnel evaluating background investigation files against national criteria to grant, deny, or suspend personnel security eligibility. Holds high USG trust.
  • CDSE Trainers: Center for Development of Security Excellence instructional staff providing standardized education and professional certifications to industry. Holds USG authority.
  • Foreign Interest: Foreign individuals, governments, commercial entities, or financial sources holding ownership, debt, board seats, or foreign-interest amounts links to the company. Positioned as untrusted by default under the FOCI evaluation framework.
  • Uncleared Staff / Academic Partners: Non-cleared corporate employees or research collaborators operating adjacent to physical or logical security perimeters. Controlled by physical and access-control boundaries.
  • Cleared Individual: Employee who has completed personnel background vetting and received a favorable adjudicative eligibility determination. Holds cleared trust, subject to Continuous Vetting monitoring.

Ten Core Mission Capabilities (Vendor-Neutral: C1–C10)

  • C1: Sponsorship Intake: Validates legitimate contractual need for classified access from a GCA or currently cleared contractor. Enforces the rule: companies cannot self-sponsor into the NISP.
  • C2: Facility Package Assembly: Aggregates corporate legal formation records, stock ledgers, capitalization tables, ownership charts, KMP rosters, and initial FOCI disclosures.
  • C3: FOCI Assessment & Mitigation: Analyzes foreign financial, operational, structural, and governance ties; evaluates risk thresholds and executes binding legal mitigation instruments.
  • C4: KMP Personnel Initiation: Extracts security-affecting corporate roles from the facility track and projects designated individuals directly into the personnel vetting pipeline.
  • C5: Facility Eligibility Grant & Sustainment: Grants formal FCL eligibility and sustains it via recurring ISR oversight, annual security self-inspections, and material change reporting.
  • C6: Personnel Background Investigation: Executes background investigations tailored to specific vetting tiers (Tier 3 for Secret/Confidential; Tier 5 for Top Secret/SCI).
  • C7: Personnel Adjudication: Applies the thirteen SEAD 4 adjudicative guidelines under the whole-person concept to render national security eligibility decisions.
  • C8: Continuous Vetting: Assesses automated real-time data signals and periodic questionnaire updates, eliminating static periodic reinvestigations for cleared NISP personnel.
  • C9: Contractor Information System Authorization: Assesses and authorizes contractor systems processing classified data using NIST SP 800-37 RMF overlaid by DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) controls.
  • C10: Counterintelligence & Insider-Threat Reporting: Collects, processes, and escalates adverse information, anomalous behavioral indicators, and unauthorized foreign contact signals.

Operational Concept: Dual-Track Swimlane Execution

  1. Phase Gate 1: Contractual Access Need Validation (Sponsorship): A GCA or Cleared Prime identifies a legitimate classified requirement (documented via DD Form 254) and submits formal sponsorship into the government facility clearing system. Self-sponsorship is strictly prohibited.
  2. Phase Gate 2: Facility Track Initiation & Clock Start (Day 1): Receipt of sponsorship welcome notification starts the Day-1 clock. The FSO initiates facility package assembly (DD Form 441, SF 328, legal organizational documents, ownership structure, KMP roster).
  3. Phase Gate 3: Facility Package Completeness Review (Day 20): The facility package must be submitted in full for completeness evaluation. Incomplete packages cycle back with limited resubmit attempts ([verify] exact attempt limit).
  4. Phase Gate 4: KMP Interlock Initiation (DCSA due-date): The KMP roster defines the subset of leadership exercising security-affecting authority. By DCSA due-date, all designated KMPs must submit background questionnaires (SF-86 class) and electronic fingerprint sets, initiating the Personnel Vetting Track.
  5. Phase Gate 5: FOCI Assessment & Mitigation Branch: Concurrently, the FOCI Certificate (SF 328) is evaluated. If foreign ownership, debt, board seats, or revenue foreign-interest amounts exceed acceptable parameters, the case branches into formal FOCI legal mitigation, extending the processing timeline by months.
  6. Phase Gate 6: Personnel Investigation & Adjudication: KMP questionnaires undergo Tier 3 or Tier 5 background investigation and SEAD 4 whole-person adjudication by government adjudicators.
  7. Phase Gate 7: Interlocked FCL Determination: Upon achieving a cleared or legally mitigated FOCI evaluation, a complete facility package, and favorable adjudicative decisions for all required KMPs, DCSA issues the formal Facility Clearance.
  8. Phase Gate 8: Operational Access & Sustainment: Controlled access is granted. Systems processing classified data execute the DAAG/RMF path (C9) to achieve a classified ATO. Cleared personnel enter Continuous Vetting (C8) and ongoing CI reporting (C10).

2.2 Business Process Architecture & Operational Activities (TOGAF Phase B / DoDAF OV-5b — Logical Level)

Sequential FCL Lifecycle Workflow & Operational Benchmarks

  • Day 1 (Sponsorship Welcome): Official notification of accepted sponsorship arrives from DCSA. The administrative clock starts.
  • Day 1 to Day 5: FSO accesses instructional resources and system workspaces to align organizational documentation to corporate legal structure.
  • Day 10 to Day 20 (Facility Package Assembly & Submission): FSO collects and uploads DD Form 441 (DoD Security Agreement), SF 328 (Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interests), legal formation articles, stock ledgers, board minutes, ownership hierarchy, and the initial KMP roster.
  • Completeness Review Cycles: DCSA reviews the submission. If deficiencies exist, the package enters a completeness cycle.
  • Constraint Note: Package completeness cycles allow limited resubmit attempts; organizations must avoid burning attempt limits on avoidable document omissions ([verify] exact allowed resubmit count limit in current handbook).
  • Day 20 to DCSA due-date (KMP Investigation Initiation & Field Review): DCSA Industrial Security Representatives (ISRs) evaluate the corporate structure and validate the KMP list. All KMPs holding security-affecting authority must complete fingerprinting and background questionnaire submissions by DCSA due-date.
  • FOCI Mitigation Branch Execution: If SF 328 evaluation reveals unmitigated foreign ties, the workflow branches to the DCSA FOCI authority. Negotiating and executing legal mitigation instruments adds substantial time (often months) to the sequence.
  • FCL Issuance & Operational Baseline Metrics: Upon satisfying all facility, legal, KMP, and FOCI requirements, DCSA issues the formal FCL level (Secret or Top Secret).
  • Overall Average FCL Processing Timeline: ~180 days from initial package acceptance to clearance grant under standard processing conditions.
  • Clean, Low-Risk FCL Timeline: ~45 days when required PCLs are already active, documentation contains zero errors, and no FOCI triggers exist.
  • Sustainment: Within the first year, DCSA conducts baseline security reviews. FCLs remain active indefinitely provided contractual need persists and compliance is maintained.

Sequential PCL Lifecycle Workflow & Timeline Metrics

  1. Access Need Identification: An eligible facility identifies an employee requiring access to classified information based on contract security requirements (DD Form 254).
  2. Questionnaire Completion: The subject completes an SF-86-class personal security questionnaire detailing 7-to-10-year personal history, foreign contacts, financial records, and legal history.
  3. Investigation Execution: DCSA executes a background investigation scoped by tier: * Tier 3 (Secret / Confidential): Scoped for Secret and Confidential access. Average processing timeline: ~56 days. * Tier 5 (Top Secret / SCI): Scoped for Top Secret and specialized access, featuring deeper historical coverage and expanded field checks. Average processing timeline: ~80 days.
  4. SEAD 4 Adjudication: Government adjudicators evaluate the investigation file against the thirteen SEAD 4 Adjudicative Guidelines under a whole-person concept (weighing favorable and unfavorable mitigation factors in aggregate).
  5. Continuous Vetting (CV) Cadence: Upon eligibility grant, the individual is enrolled in Continuous Vetting. Automated data sources are monitored continuously. * Policy Clarification (May 2026 NISP Update): Static Periodic Reinvestigations (PRs) have been officially eliminated for NISP contractors in favor of continuous monitoring. Personnel submit an updated Personnel Vetting Questionnaire (PVQ) on a mandatory 5-year cycle.

Five Legal FOCI Mitigation Instruments

When SF 328 disclosures reveal foreign ownership, control, or influence exceeding administrative thresholds, DCSA requires execution of one of five binding legal mitigation instruments to neutralize foreign risk: 1. Board Resolution: Utilized when a foreign interest owns voting stock but does not possess executive power or board representation; the corporate board adopts resolutions excluding the foreign entity from access to classified info and KMP decisions. 2. Security Control Agreement (SCA): Utilized when a company is not effectively owned or controlled by a foreign entity, but a foreign interest holds significant voting stock and representation on the board of directors. 3. Special Security Agreement (SSA): Utilized when a US entity is effectively owned or controlled by a foreign interest; requires US citizen board members, strict isolation controls, and GCA National Interest Determinations (NID) for Top Secret access. 4. Voting Trust Agreement (VTA): Utilized when foreign owners transfer all voting rights of their stock to cleared US citizen Trustees who operate the company independently. 5. Proxy Agreement (PA): Utilized when foreign owners surrender complete voting rights and operational control to cleared US citizen Proxy Holders, creating total separation between foreign owners and corporate management.

Logical Interlock Mechanism

The FCL track cannot complete without the PCL track. The KMP Roster generated in the facility track defines the mandatory subset of individuals who must enter the PCL track. If a designated KMP is denied eligibility or fails to initiate investigation by DCSA due-date, the facility clearance workflow halts. Conversely, adverse information surfaced in the personnel track regarding a KMP can suspend or revoke the facility's overall clearance.

3. TOGAF ADM Phase C (Information Systems Architecture) / DoDAF Data Viewpoints (DIV-1, DIV-2, DIV-3)

3.1 Conceptual Data Model (DoDAF DIV-1 — Conceptual Level)

Core Architectural Concepts

  • Facility Clearance (FCL): An administrative determination that a commercial or academic legal entity is structurally, legally, and operationally eligible to access classified information at a specified security level (Confidential, Secret, Top Secret).
  • Personnel Clearance (PCL): An administrative determination that an individual is eligible for access to classified information up to a specified sensitivity level.
  • Sponsorship: The mandatory formal mechanism by which a GCA or an existing cleared Prime contractor certifies a legitimate contractual need for an uncleared entity to enter the NISP. Self-sponsorship is strictly prohibited.
  • Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI): A condition where a foreign interest has direct or indirect power (exercised or exercisable) to decide or direct operational, managerial, or technical matters of a US entity, creating risk of unauthorized access or contract compromise.
  • Possessing vs. Non-Possessing Facilities:
  • Possessing Facility: Authorized to physically receive, store, and process classified material on-site. Requires physical safeguards (vaults, closed areas, approved containers) and specialized ISSM oversight.
  • Non-Possessing Facility: Cleared to perform classified work on-site at USG or customer locations. Holds no physical or logical classified holdings on its own premises.
  • SEAD 4 Whole-Person Guidelines: The mandate that personnel eligibility determinations must aggregate thirteen adjudicative themes (e.g., allegiance, foreign influence, financial considerations, personal conduct) into a holistic assessment rather than applying single automatic disqualifiers.

Textual Entity Relationship Description

The foundational entity is the NISP Facility Record. It is legally bound to the USG via the DD 441 Agreement and certifies its foreign risk posture using the SF 328 Certificate. The facility's corporate legal hierarchy is proved by the Ownership Chart.

Operational facility execution requires populating a KMP Roster. Each slot designated as exercising security-affecting authority initiates a PCL Case, which collects an SF-86-class Questionnaire. The background investigation yields an Adjudication Record. If foreign control triggers are identified on the SF 328, one of five FOCI Mitigation Instruments (Board Resolution, SCA, SSA, Voting Trust, or Proxy Agreement) is bound to the facility record. Facilities processing classified data attach a System Authorization Package. Workforce security competency is verified via Training Completion Records.

4. DoDAF Operational Resource Flow (OV-2) & System Interface Matrix (SV-1, SV-6)

4.1 Inter-Agency Operational Resource Flow (DoDAF OV-2 — Logical-to-Physical)

The operational resource flow spans organizational boundaries, connecting the Government Contracting Activity (GCA), the cleared contractor, and DCSA enterprise systems:

  1. GCA / Cleared Prime $\rightarrow$ NISS: Transmits contract sponsorship data, CAGE code, and contract security requirements via DD Form 254 to open the facility case.
  2. Contractor FSO $\rightarrow$ NISS: Transmits executed DD Form 441, updated SF 328, corporate legal structure files, ownership charts, and KMP rosters.
  3. KMP / Vetting Subject $\rightarrow$ eApp (DISS): Transmits completed SF-86-class questionnaires and electronic biometrics (fingerprint files) to launch background investigations.
  4. Contractor ISSM $\rightarrow$ eMASS (or A&A Repository): Transmits RMF accreditation artifacts, system boundary definitions, and control evidence using the DAAG overlay.
  5. Corporate HR $\rightarrow$ Contractor FSO: Transmits internal employee adverse information reports, legal status changes, or foreign travel logs over an audited internal handoff path.
  6. Contractor FSO $\rightarrow$ Personnel Eligibility SoR (DISS — [verify]): Submits formal reportable adverse information incidents, foreign travel, and contact reports to government adjudicators.

4.3 AI Elements & Integration Boundary Constraints (Physical / Integration Layer)

Affirmative AI Boundary

Artificial Intelligence tools may operate only in an assistive capacity for non-PII, unclassified workflows. Permitted operational use cases include: * Assembling non-PII facility package submission checklists based on public handbooks. * Summarizing public CDSE educational course outlines (e.g., IS140, IS142). * Formatting draft organizational ownership charts using pre-cleared, non-sensitive legal summaries. * Running local, disconnected spreadsheet calculations to flag Q7(b) reporting approaching the 15% aggregate line.

Hard-Block Control Path

An absolute architectural restriction governs all AI integrations: SF-86-class sensitive PII, biometric fingerprint data, and SF 328 FOCI disclosures are strictly forbidden from entering casual, commercial, or unaccredited consumer AI models. * Default Deny Control: Any internal prompt or workflow tool attempting to transmit personnel security questionnaires or cap-table foreign ties to external AI APIs must be hard-blocked at the perimeter. * Processing Boundary: SF-86 and FOCI data must reside exclusively within government-operated systems (NISS, NBIS, Personnel Eligibility SoR) or corporate repositories accredited for sensitive PII.

Non-Substitution Rule

AI tools do not sponsor companies into the NISP, do not make SEAD 4 adjudicative determinations, and do not issue FOCI decisions. All governance decisions remain exclusively human responsibilities executed by authorized USG officials.

Facility Physical Baseline: Possessing vs. Non-Possessing

  • Possessing Facility: Physical infrastructure built to handle classified material on-site. Requires physical security perimeters, GSA-approved containers, closed areas, secure processing rooms, visitor control logs, and an assigned ISSM overseeing local classified IT enclaves.
  • Non-Possessing Facility: Facility cleared to allow employees to access classified material at customer or USG sites. Physical premises contain no classified storage or processing. Educational compliance follows the CDSE IS020.CU Non-Possessing FSO Curriculum path (26.5 hours of instruction including IS140 and IS142).

5.2 Standards Profile & Compliance Frameworks (DoDAF StdV-1 — Physical Level)

Primary Mandatory Standards Baseline

  1. 32 CFR Part 117 (NISPOM): The federal regulation governing the National Industrial Security Program, defining baseline security obligations for cleared industry.
  2. SEAD 4 (Security Executive Agent Directive 4): National adjudicative guidelines establishing the thirteen criteria and whole-person concept for personnel security eligibility.
  3. DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) (DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual): The mandatory implementation manual overlaying the NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF) for contractor classified information systems.
  4. NIST SP 800-37 / RMF: System security authorization standards applied to contractor enclaves as overlaid by DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG).
  5. Mandatory Core DoD Forms: * DD Form 254: Contract Security Classification Specification (defines contractual access need). * DD Form 441: Department of Defense Security Agreement (binds corporate entity to NISPOM). * SF 328: Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interests (May 2025 revision; measures FOCI). * SF-86: Questionnaire for National Security Positions (underpins Tier 3/Tier 5 investigations).

Non-Substitute Regimes & Decision Rules

Architectural blueprints must explicitly enforce the following decision rules disallowing commercial/unclassified accreditations as substitutes for NISP requirements:

  • CMMC (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification): Decision Rule: CMMC SP 800-171 is an unclassified CUI cybersecurity floor; it provides zero administrative eligibility for NISP facility access.
  • FedRAMP / GCC High (Government Cloud Enclaves): Decision Rule: FedRAMP, GCC High, and DoD Cloud Impact Levels (IL4/IL5) accredit hosting infrastructure posture; they do not sponsor a facility into NISS, satisfy SF 328 FOCI disclosures, or grant an FCL.
  • DoD Cloud Impact Levels (IL4 / IL5): Decision Rule: Processing, storing, or transmitting classified material on contractor systems requires a formal classified ATO under DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) and NIST SP 800-37 RMF, separate from commercial cloud IL attestations.
