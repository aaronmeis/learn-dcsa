Executive Briefing: DCSA Industrial & Personnel Security Governance Operating Model

1. Executive Summary

The Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) functions as the primary security agency and national gatekeeper for the United States Department of Defense (DOD) and federal executive branch industrial base. Overseeing security operations across 167 field locations with approximately 10,000 personnel, DCSA enforces security oversight for 33 federal agencies, over 10,000 cleared companies, and 12,500 contractor facilities—65% of which are small businesses.

Industrial security is governed through two interlocking, mutually dependent operational tracks: 1. Facility Clearance (FCL) governed by the National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual (NISPOM), codifying regulation 32 CFR Part 117, managed via the National Industrial Security System (NISS). 2. Personnel Security Clearance (PCL) managed through the National Background Investigation System (NBIS) and Electronic Questionnaires for Investigations Processing (eApp) / Standard Form 86 (SF-86), adjudicated under Security Executive Agent Directive 4 (SEAD 4) guidelines.

Critical Operating Constraints & Falsifiers

  • The Dual-Track Lock: An organization cannot establish an operational PCL pipeline without an active, eligible facility clearance sponsor; conversely, an FCL cannot be issued or maintained without cleared Key Management Personnel (KMP) possessing a verified need-to-know.
  • Absence of Self-Sponsorship: A commercial contractor or laboratory cannot self-sponsor an FCL. Initial sponsorship must be formally executed in NISS by a Government Contracting Activity (GCA) or an established currently cleared contractor contractor.
  • Regulatory & Systemic Separation ("Two Walls"): Security frameworks for Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)—such as CMMC 2.0, FedRAMP, or Microsoft GCC High—do not equal an FCL and do not authorize classified processing (Secret/Top Secret). Systems processing classified information must be authorized under the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAPM v2.2) (historical; now the DAAG) executing the Risk Management Framework (RMF).
  • May 2025 SF 328 Foreign Revenue Threshold Rule: Standard Form 328 (Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interests) lowers the Question 7b reporting threshold to 15%, significantly expanding Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) disclosure requirements for entities with foreign research funding, grants, or international commercial revenue.

2. Agency Architecture & Four Core Security Domains

Following congressional directives in the 2018 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) prompted by the 2015 Office of Personnel Management (OPM) data breach, DCSA underwent a major structural consolidation. On October 1, 2019, OPM's National Background Investigation Bureau (NBIB), DOD's Defense Security Service (DSS), and the DOD Consolidated Adjudications Facility (DOD CAF) merged into DCSA. On October 1, 2020, four additional transfers occurred: OPM/DISA legacy background IT infrastructure, the National Background Investigation System (NBIS), Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC) security systems, and the National Center for Credibility Assessment (NCCA) from the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA). In FY22, DCSA also assumed responsibility for DIA Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SCIF) accreditation.

DCSA operates across four interlocking domains:

  1. Industrial Security / National Industrial Security Program (NISP): Administers FCLs, DD Form 441 (Security Agreements), SF 328 FOCI reviews, and Industrial Security Representative (ISR) facility oversight under 32 CFR Part 117.
  2. Personnel Vetting: Manages background investigations (Tiers 3 and 5), eApp (DISS) processing, SEAD 4 adjudications, and the transition to Continuous Vetting (CV).
  3. Information Systems Authorization: Executes Assessment and Authorization (A&A) under DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) / RMF for classified contractor information systems.
  4. Counterintelligence (CI): Directs insider threat mitigation, foreign travel/contact reporting, and threat identification through integrated units like the DOD Insider Threat Management and Analysis Center (DITMAC).

3. Track 1: Facility Security Clearance (FCL) Execution Mechanics

Sponsorship & NISS Intake

A company cannot request an FCL independently. Sponsorship requires a verified requirement for classified access tied to a specific solicitation or awarded contract. A GCA or currently cleared contractor initiates the request in NISS by submitting a DD Form 254 (Contract Security Classification Specification). Pre-award sponsorship is permitted only if access to classified information is mandatory during the bidding/solicitation phase or when award is imminent.

Mandatory Clocks & Package Checklist

Upon acceptance of sponsorship, DCSA issues a welcome email. Day 1 of the operational clock begins upon receipt:

Complete FCL Package Contents:

  • Legal Formation Documents: Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws, Operating Agreements, Partnership Agreements, or Business Licenses matching sam.gov and CAGE code records.
  • Ownership / Org Chart: Explicit breakdown of ultimate beneficial owners, holding companies, parent entities, and voting stock distribution.
  • DD Form 441 (DOD Security Agreement): Legally binding contract between the entity and the US Government agreeing to comply with NISPOM (32 CFR Part 117) obligations.
  • SF 328 (Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interests): Standardized 10-question disclosure of foreign ownership, control, influence, revenue, and indebtedness.
  • Key Management Personnel (KMP) List: Identified roles defining leadership with legal and operational authority over classified performance.
  • Fingerprints & Investigation Readiness: Scheduled eApp (DISS) submissions for all uncleared KMPs.

Note on Execution Bottlenecks: First-time package submissions are frequently rejected by DCSA due to structural document discrepancies, mismatched CAGE/SAM addresses, or incomplete KMP definitions. Documentation quality and horizontal/vertical consistency across all forms are the primary determinants of cycle time.

Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) Oversight

Under 32 CFR Part 117, an entity is under FOCI whenever a foreign interest has direct or indirect power (exercised or unexercised) to direct or decide operational, managerial, or classified performance decisions.

The May 2025 SF 328 Policy Update

  • Approval & NISS Deployment: Form approved May 1, 2025; live in NISS on approximately May 12, 2025.
  • Question 7b Threshold Shift: The foreign revenue and financial disclosure threshold is reduced to 15%.
  • Operational Impact: Contractors, research institutions, and university labs deriving 15% or more of total annual revenue or foreign-interest amounts from foreign sources (foreign grants, international research partnerships, foreign commercial clients) must submit full FOCI narratives.
  • Schedule Risk & Mitigations: If unmitigated FOCI exists, formal agreements—such as Security Control Agreements (SCAs), Special Security Agreements (SSAs), Voting Trusts, or Proxy Agreements—must be negotiated with DCSA. FOCI mitigation typically adds 6 to 12 months to the FCL approval timeline.
  • Non-Permanence Principle: "No FOCI" status is not static. Any material change in corporate ownership, foreign debt, foreign board members, or foreign revenue crossing the 15% threshold requires an immediate SF 328 update submission in NISS.

Facility Types: Possessing vs. Non-Possessing

  • Non-Possessing FCL: The contractor requires access to classified information, but storage and physical performance occur entirely at a government or prime contractor site. Non-possessing facilities still require full GCA sponsorship, an appointed FSO, cleared KMPs, a DD-441, an SF 328, and a verified security program.
  • Possessing FCL: The contractor physically receives, stores, generates, or holds classified material on site. Requires physical security infrastructure inspections (safes, closed areas, access controls) by DCSA Industrial Security Representatives (ISRs) and formal authorization of classified IT systems under DAAG/RMF. FCL clearances apply to specific geographic locations and CAGE codes.

SEAD 4 Adjudicative Guidelines (Whole-Person Concept)

Adjudication decisions rendered by the DCSA Consolidated Adjudications Facility (CAF) evaluate eligibility using the 13 whole-person guidelines under Security Executive Agent Directive 4 (SEAD 4). Adjudication is cumulative and contextual, weighing conduct, recency, motivation, and mitigation, rather than executing a rigid checklist.

  1. Guideline A: Allegiance to the United States
  2. Guideline B: Foreign Influence
  3. Guideline C: Foreign Preference
  4. Guideline D: Sexual Behavior
  5. Guideline E: Personal Conduct
  6. Guideline F: Financial Considerations
  7. Guideline G: Alcohol Consumption
  8. Guideline H: Drug Involvement and Substance Misuse
  9. Guideline I: Psychological Conditions
  10. Guideline J: Criminal Conduct
  11. Guideline K: Handling Protected Information
  12. Guideline L: Outside Activities
  13. Guideline M: Use of Information Technology Systems

Adjudication Reality: Past personal infractions (e.g., historical substance use, financial delinquencies, minor criminal records) are not automatic disqualifiers. The CAF evaluates transparency, passage of time, and personal reform. Non-disclosure or deliberate falsification on the SF-86 (Guideline E) is significantly more punitive than the underlying issue itself.

Continuous Vetting (CV) Transition

DCSA has largely phased out traditional Periodic Reinvestigations (PRs) every 5 or 10 years for NISP contractors in favor of Continuous Vetting (CV): * Cleared personnel are enrolled in automated systems that continuously check commercial, financial, law enforcement, and public record data feeds in real time for risk indicators. * Personnel Security Questionnaires (PVQ) are refreshed on a 5-year cadence, but periodic reinvestigation field sweeps have been eliminated for standard maintenance.

5. Information Systems Authorization & Cyber Framework Alignment

A primary point of operational failure for defense contractors is confusing unclassified cybersecurity compliance with classified system authorization.

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        UNCLASSIFIED CUI FLOOR                          │
│  Controls: NIST SP 800-171 / CMMC Level 2 / FedRAMP / GCC High          │
│  Scope: Unclassified CUI, proprietary technical data, sub-tier IT      │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │
                        DOES NOT CONFER FCL OR ATO
                                    │
┌───────────────────────────────────▼────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        CLASSIFIED NISP ATO WALL                        │
│  Controls: DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) executing RMF (NIST SP 800-53)                   │
│  Scope: Secret / Top Secret processing, possessing facility networks   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  • The CMMC / GCC High Floor: Frameworks such as CMMC 2.0 (NIST SP 800-171), FedRAMP, or Microsoft GCC High establish security controls for unclassified CUI. They do not authorize the storage, processing, or transmission of classified information.
  • The DAAG / RMF Wall: Processing classified information inside a possessing contractor facility requires a formal Authority to Operate (ATO) issued by DCSA under the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAPM v2.2) (historical; now the DAAG), which implements the DOD Risk Management Framework (RMF).
  • Coursework Mapping: FSOs managing classified IT systems must complete CDSE course CS250.16 (Applying Assessment and Authorization in the NISP) and implement DAAG requirements alongside physical security controls.

7. Contextual Analysis of Key Sourced Statements

On DCSA's National Mandate

"Make no mistake, from a security perspective, DCSA is the nation's gatekeeper, and we fulfill that role through a variety of security functions... Over 2.6 million background investigations for over 100 federal departments per year... oversight for 33 federal agencies, approximately 10,000 clear companies, and 12,500 contractor facilities."
— Steve Mapes, Director for Small Business Programs, DCSA

Contextual Significance: Illustrates the central role DCSA holds across the federal government. Industrial security is not an internal corporate HR process; it is a federal oversight mechanism managed by a single agency enforcing standardized national security standards across public and private domains.

On the Rule of Sponsorship

"A company cannot apply for its own FCLs; they must be sponsored by either a Government Contracting Agency (GCA) or a cleared contractor... The government will not grant an FCL unless and until the applicant has a need to access classified information... somebody has to validate that need."
— Steve Mapes, Director for Small Business Programs, DCSA

Contextual Significance: Dispel the persistent commercial myth of "self-sponsorship." The sponsorship requirement ensures that federal security clearances remain tied to verified contractual necessity, preventing speculative or commercial misuse of national security clearance infrastructure.

On Asymmetrical Supply Chain Threats

"Our competitors, China... Russia... they know you're important... They're not going to bomb our factories; they're going to buy our factories, or they're going to offer up a joint venture that looks appealing on the outside, but what they're trying to do is get inside... to leverage the things we're doing."
— Dr. Mears, Deputy Director of Logistics (A4), Headquarters Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)

Contextual Significance: Frame FOCI enforcement as an active defense countermeasure against asymmetrical economic warfare. Foreign state-backed acquisition of sub-tier defense suppliers represents a strategic vector to steal intellectual property, introduce counterfeit components, or disrupt defense supply chains.

On Disclosure and Voluntary Compliance

"Bad news ages like milk... Non-disclosure is way more punitive than voluntary disclosure. If you don't believe so, just go out and look at the difference in findings between self-identified ITAR violations and the ones that they found out... Self-disclosure gets you a workable relationship where they can build mitigations."
— Mike Hoover, Supply Chain Risk Management (SCRUM) Lead, AFMC

Contextual Significance: Establishes the operational principle for FSOs and executive leadership when managing security incidents, FOCI changes, or clearance delinquencies. Active, timely reporting enables risk mitigation, whereas concealment typically results in clearance revocation, facility suspension, or legal debarment.

9. Actionable Implementation Playbook

Phase 1: Pre-Sponsorship Preparation (30 Days Prior)

  1. Corporate Records Audit: Verify that company legal name, address, CAGE code, and SAM.gov profile match across all legal documentation.
  2. KMP Identification: Establish a legal KMP list. Appoint the Senior Management Official (SMO), Facility Security Officer (FSO), and Insider Threat Program Senior Official (ITPSO).
  3. FOCI Pre-Screening: Conduct an internal accounting review of all foreign revenue, grants, international joint ventures, and capitalization tables against the May 2025 SF 328 15% Q7b threshold.
  4. PKI Credential Acquisition: Obtain commercial Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) certificates or DOD Common Access Cards (CAC) required for administrative access to NISS and NBIS.

Phase 2: Post-Sponsorship Execution (Days 1–45 Clock)

  1. Day 1 Intake: Receive DCSA Welcome Email. Confirm CAGE code and level of clearance requested (Secret vs. Top Secret).
  2. Days 1–15 Execution: Complete eApp (DISS) registration. Draft DD Form 441 and SF 328 disclosures.
  3. Day 20 Governance Gate: Upload legal formation docs, ownership charts, signed DD-441, completed SF 328, and official KMP list into NISS.
  4. Days 21–40 Execution: Schedule digital fingerprinting and SF-86 completion for all uncleared KMPs.
  5. DCSA due-date KMP Gate: Ensure all mandatory KMP investigation requests and fingerprint files are submitted in NBIS.

Phase 3: Post-Grant Operational Maintenance

  1. Establish Insider Threat Program: Issue formal appointment letters for FSO and ITPSO. Publish insider threat policy statement.
  2. Implement Continuous Reporting: Establish mandatory reporting workflows for material corporate changes, foreign travel, foreign contacts, adverse candidate information, and SF 328 updates.
  3. Annual Compliance Cycle: Perform required NISP annual self-inspections utilizing CDSE checklists prior to formal DCSA Industrial Security Representative (ISR) audits.
