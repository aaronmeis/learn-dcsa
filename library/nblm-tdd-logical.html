DCSA Dual-Track Operating Model: Logical Architecture View

1. Document Scope and Architectural Boundary

1.1 Mission Purpose and Dual-Track Scope

This document specifies the logical architecture view for the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) industrial security and personnel vetting operating model. The architecture establishes the functional baseline for executing the National Industrial Security Program (NISP), codified under 32 CFR Part 117, across cleared defense industry entities. The core operational structure relies on a dual-track framework: Facility Clearance (FCL) eligibility interlocking directly with Personnel Clearance (PCL) eligibility. The purpose of this logical view is to define functional boundaries, logical data structures, inter-component communication flows, temporal gates, and operational decision rules required to achieve and sustain security eligibility across cleared industry.

Note

The dual-track operating model is structurally interdependent: neither the Facility Clearance (FCL) track nor the Personnel Clearance (PCL) track is sufficient on its own. An organization cannot hold an active facility clearance without cleared Key Management Personnel (KMP), and individual personnel clearances cannot be granted without a verified contractual access need anchored to a sponsored facility. Both tracks structurally feed, validate, and depend on each other throughout the industrial security lifecycle.

1.2 Logical Altitude and Vendor Neutrality Guidelines

This specification operates strictly at the Logical Architecture View altitude. It defines what functional components perform work, what data entities exist, and how information moves across administrative boundaries, completely independent of specific hosting providers, hardware platforms, commercial software titles, or physical portal product names.

To maintain architectural neutrality and conceptual rigor, the following boundary rules are strictly enforced:

  • In-Scope Logical Domains:

    • Domain 1 (D1): Industrial Security / NISP: Facility sponsorship intake, package assembly, completeness validation, Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) evaluation, and facility eligibility sustainment.
    • Domain 2 (D2): Personnel Vetting: Key Management Personnel (KMP) initiation, background investigation scoping, SEAD 4 adjudicative processing, and Continuous Vetting (CV).
    • Domain 3 (D3): Information Systems Authorization: Contractor classified system authorization under the Risk Management Framework (RMF) utilizing the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAG) agency overlay.
    • Domain 4 (D4): Counterintelligence (CI): Insider threat monitoring, adverse information ingestion, and foreign contact reporting.

  • Out-of-Scope Elements:

    • Physical portal product names and legacy platform titles (e.g., NISS, NBIS, DISS, eMASS, STEPP).
    • Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) control-level memorization (CMMC serves as a Controlled Unclassified Information [CUI] cybersecurity floor, not an FCL or classified ATO).
    • Commercial Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) vendor product tours or feature reviews.
    • Special Access Program (SAP) and Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) enclave physical engineering.

A NISP Facility Record serves as the primary anchor entity. It maintains a strict 1-to-1 relationship with the DD Form 441 Agreement (establishing legal program participation) and the SF 328 Certificate (certifying foreign interest exposure). The SF 328 Certificate is further explained by an Ownership Chart containing one or more corporate hierarchy nodes (1-to-1..M). If the SF 328 Certificate or Ownership Chart discloses foreign control elements exceeding operational safety thresholds, a FOCI Mitigation Instrument is generated and attached to the NISP Facility Record (1-to-0..1).

The NISP Facility Record defines a 1-to-Many relationship with the KMP Roster. Each individual slot on the KMP Roster holding security-affecting authority initiates a personal eligibility request, creating a 1-to-1 binding between a KMP Roster Slot and a PCL Case. The PCL Case ingests a completed SF-86-Class Questionnaire (1-to-1), which triggers an investigation resulting in an Adjudication Record (1-to-0..1). If the Adjudication Record yields an adverse decision for a required KMP, the underlying NISP Facility Record cannot achieve full clearance eligibility unless that KMP is legally excluded from security-affecting authority. Furthermore, for facilities designated as possessing, the NISP Facility Record maintains a conditional 1-to-Many binding (1-to-0..M) with System Authorization Packages.

Summary of Cardinalities: * NISP Facility Record to DD Form 441 Agreement: 1-to-1 * NISP Facility Record to SF 328 Certificate: 1-to-1 * SF 328 Certificate to Ownership Chart Nodes: 1-to-1..M * NISP Facility Record to KMP Roster Slots: 1-to-Many * KMP Roster Slot to PCL Case: 1-to-1 (for positions holding security-affecting authority) * PCL Case to SF-86-Class Questionnaire: 1-to-1 * PCL Case to Adjudication Record: 1-to-0..1 (Conditional upon investigation completion) * NISP Facility Record to FOCI Mitigation Instrument: 1-to-0..1 (Conditional upon FOCI determination) * NISP Facility Record to System Authorization Package: 1-to-0..M (Conditional based on possessing status) * PCL Case to Training Completion Record: 1-to-Many

4.2 Facility Clearance (FCL) Lifecycle and Hard Clocks

Achieving facility eligibility follows a structured logical progression subject to enforced temporal gates:

  1. Day 1 (Sponsorship Acceptance): A Government Contracting Activity (GCA) or an existing cleared Prime Contractor submits a valid sponsorship request establishing a legitimate contractual need for classified access via DD Form 254 context. The Sponsorship Gate validates the need, accepts the request, issues a welcome notification, and starts the master clock.
  2. Day 20 (Package Assembly & Completeness Gate): The Facility Package Assembler aggregates the company's core organizational artifacts (DD Form 441, SF 328, Ownership Chart, formation documents, and KMP Roster) and submits the bundle to the Completeness Reviewer.
  3. Completeness Validation Cycles: The Completeness Reviewer evaluates the package for horizontal and vertical consistency across all documents. If deficiencies exist, the package is returned for corrective action within strict resubmission attempt limits.
  4. FOCI Evaluation & Branching: Upon completeness acceptance, the FOCI Analyzer evaluates foreign ownership, foreign-interest amounts, and employment. If unmitigated foreign control is present, the process branches to the Mitigation Instrument Manager.
  5. DCSA due-date (KMP Initiation Gate): All personnel designated on the KMP Roster who hold security-affecting authority must complete their SF-86-Class Questionnaires and submit electronic biometrics to the KMP Initiation Engine.
  6. Facility Eligibility Grant: Once the package is verified complete, FOCI is cleared or mitigated, and required KMPs achieve initial personnel eligibility, the Facility Eligibility Authority issues the formal Facility Clearance determination.
  7. Sustainment & Oversight: The facility enters the sustainment phase, requiring continuous self-inspections, material change reporting, and periodic oversight reviews.
Warning

Architectural Clocks & Gates * Day 20 Clock (Facility Package Gate): The organization must submit a fully execution-ready facility package—including the SF 328, DD Form 441, Ownership Chart, and KMP Roster—within 20 calendar days of sponsorship acceptance. Day 20 Failure Mode: Exhausting allowed completeness resubmission cycles due to document discrepancies or missing artifacts leads to immediate package rejection and sponsorship termination. * DCSA due-date Clock (KMP Initiation Gate): All key leadership personnel identified on the KMP Roster holding security-affecting authority must complete questionnaire data input and electronic biometric submissions within 45 calendar days of sponsorship acceptance. DCSA due-date Failure Mode: Failure of designated KMPs to submit questionnaires and biometrics halts the FCL eligibility grant and risks sponsorship revocation. * FOCI Mitigation Schedule Impact: If the FOCI Analyzer determines that foreign ownership, control, or influence requires a formal legal instrument (e.g., Voting Trust, Proxy Agreement, SSA, SCA, or Board Resolution), executing and verifying the instrument automatically expands the lifecycle schedule by months.

4.3 Personnel Clearance (PCL) Lifecycle and KMP Interlock

The Personnel Clearance lifecycle interfaces directly with the facility track through the Key Management Personnel roster:

  1. Initiation:
    • A candidate or KMP's access requirement is established via an approved KMP Roster slot or a contract security specification (DD Form 254).
    • The KMP Initiation Engine issues processing credentials to the subject.
    • The subject submits a completed SF-86-Class Questionnaire along with electronic fingerprint biometric payloads.
  2. Scoped Investigation:
    • The Investigation Case Manager ingests the submission and scopes the investigation according to the requested access level.
    • Tier 3 Investigations: Conducted for Secret / Confidential access needs, focusing on standard 5-year to 7-year historical reporting windows.
    • Tier 5 Investigations: Conducted for Top Secret access needs, executing expanded 10-year historical background checks, field interviews, and deep financial checks.
  3. SEAD 4 Adjudication:
    • Upon investigation completion, the case file is transferred to the Adjudication Engine.
    • Adjudicators apply Security Executive Agent Directive 4 (SEAD 4) adjudicative criteria across thirteen (13) national security guidelines (Guidelines A through M).
    • Decision-making follows the "whole-person" concept, balancing favorable and unfavorable factors rather than applying automatic single-issue disqualifications.
  4. Continuous Vetting:
    • Upon eligibility grant, the cleared individual is enrolled in the Continuous Vetting Monitor.
    • Automated data checks continuously assess commercial, legal, and government data streams for risk indicators.
    • Under updated NISP Continuous Vetting rules (effective May 2026), cleared contractors complete a Personnel Vetting Questionnaire (PVQ) every 5 years, officially eliminating legacy Periodic Reinvestigations (PRs).
    • Any flagged risk signals trigger real-time alerts to the Adjudication Engine for review.

4.4 Special Branch Flows: FOCI Mitigation and Systems Authorization

Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) Branch

When the FOCI Analyzer evaluates an organization's SF 328 Certificate disclosures and Ownership Chart, it checks for Q7(b) reporting, foreign equity stakes, foreign board representation, or foreign employment of management. If foreign elements possess direct or indirect power to decide matters affecting entity management or contract performance, the workflow branches off the standard lifecycle path:

  • Determination Paths:
    1. Clear: Foreign involvement is non-existent or determined too negligible to impact national security interests.
    2. Mitigate: Foreign influence is present but can be effectively negated using a tailored, binding legal instrument managed by the Mitigation Instrument Manager.
    3. Deny: Foreign ownership or influence is pervasive and unmitigable, resulting in the denial of facility clearance eligibility.
  • Mitigation Instrument Types: Depending on the depth of foreign ownership and voting control, mitigation instruments are structured as Board Resolutions (for minor exposure), Security Control Agreements (SCA), Special Security Agreements (SSA), or Proxy Agreements / Voting Trusts (which fully isolate foreign owners from management and security control).

Information Systems Authorization Branch

If a facility achieves clearance eligibility as a possessing facility—requiring the processing, storage, or transmission of classified data on contractor-owned systems—it must execute the Information Systems Authorization branch flow:

  • DAAG / RMF Integration: The IS Authorization Package Manager executes the Risk Management Framework (RMF) lifecycle as adapted by the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAG) agency overlay.
  • Execution Steps:
    1. Categorization & Boundary Definition: Establish the authorization boundary for the contractor classified system.
    2. Control Selection: Apply security controls specified by NIST criteria plus specific DAAG overlay controls required for NISP contractor environments.
    3. Implementation & Assessment: Implement control baselines and document evidence within the System Security Plan (SSP).
    4. Package Submission: Submit the System Authorization Package to the Authorizing Official path to obtain a formal Authorization to Operate (ATO).

5. Decision Rules, Governance, and Silence Register

5.1 Architectural Decision Rules and Tells

The execution of the DCSA logical architecture is governed by explicit, non-negotiable architectural decision rules:

  • No Self-Sponsorship Rule: An uncleared entity cannot self-sponsor into the National Industrial Security Program. Facility clearance processing must originate strictly from a verified US Government Contracting Activity (GCA) or an existing cleared Prime Contractor with a valid, documented access requirement.
  • SF 328 Foreign Proceeds Threshold Rule: Under the updated SF 328 standard (approved May 1, deployed May 12, 2025), the Q7(b) reporting threshold under Question 7(b) is lowered to 15%. Organizations must aggregate foreign gifts, tuition, endowments, grants, and foreign management employment ties under this lower 15% line; evaluating financial exposure using pre-2025 SF 328 models constitutes an immediate architecture failure mode. Furthermore, foreign employment of management requires executing a formal Statement of Full Disclosure of Foreign Affiliations.
  • KMP Security-Affecting Authority Rule: Key Management Personnel (KMP) roster inclusion must be determined by testing actual security-affecting operational authority over classified performance or facility management, not by corporate executive job titles alone.
  • Boundary Distinction Rule: Architectural designs must clearly isolate and distinguish between three distinct security regimes:
    1. CMMC Level: Establishes a cybersecurity floor for handling Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI); it does not grant an FCL or authorize classified access.
    2. Facility Clearance (FCL): Establishes organizational administrative eligibility under NISP to access classified material.
    3. System ATO: Authorizes specific information technology systems to process classified data under DAAG/RMF rules. Commercial cloud accreditations (e.g., FedRAMP, GCC High) do not equal an FCL or a classified system ATO.
  • AI Boundary Rule: Artificial Intelligence capabilities deployed within cleared contractor operations must be restricted strictly to assistive workflows (such as non-PII checklist generation, public handbook Q&A, and basic document formatting). AI engines are strictly barred from performing self-sponsorship, SEAD 4 adjudicative decision-making, or FOCI risk determinations. Furthermore, ingesting SF-86-class Personally Identifiable Information (PII) or unredacted corporate FOCI cap-table data into non-approved commercial AI models is strictly prohibited.
