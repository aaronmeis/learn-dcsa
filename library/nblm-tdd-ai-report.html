DCSA AI Architecture, Integration Surface, and Governance Report

1. Executive Mission & AI Operational Boundary

1.1 Mission Context and Core AI Boundary Rules

The Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) administers the National Industrial Security Program (NISP) under 32 CFR Part 117, serving as the security authority and gatekeeper for cleared defense industry facilities and personnel. The DCSA operating framework is built upon a dual-track eligibility model—interlocking Facility Clearance (FCL) eligibility and Personnel Clearance (PCL) eligibility—supported by Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) evaluation, Information System (IS) authorization under the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAPM v2.2) (historical; now the DAAG) and NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF), continuous vetting (CV), and counterintelligence (CI) oversight.

NISP Lifecycle Clocks and Hard Governance Gates

The operational execution of the NISP framework is bounded by strict regulatory clocks that bind contractor actions to specific timelines upon sponsorship acceptance: * Day 1 (Sponsorship Acceptance): The sponsorship welcome email is accepted in the National Industrial Security System (NISS) following formal submission by a Government Contracting Activity (GCA) or an currently cleared contractor contractor. Self-sponsorship into the NISP is strictly prohibited. * Day 20 (Facility Package Hard Gate): The contractor must assemble and upload a complete facility clearance package into NISS. This package includes the executed Security Agreement (DD Form 441), the Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interests (SF 328), legal formation documents, board minutes, stock ledgers, entity ownership diagrams, and the Key Management Personnel (KMP) roster. Package incomplete submissions trigger completeness cycles with limited resubmit attempts ([verify]); burning these attempts through document deficiencies results in sponsorship termination. * DCSA due-date (KMP Initiation Hard Gate): All personnel designated on the KMP roster possessing security-affecting authority must complete their background investigation initiation. This requires submitting electronic questionnaires (SF-86 class via eApp (DISS)) and electronic fingerprint intake.

The SF 328 Regulatory Revision (May 2025 Parameters)

FOCI evaluation operates on updated regulatory baselines following the revised SF 328 form, approved by the Office of Management and Budget on 1 May 2025 and deployed live in NISS on 12 May 2025. Enterprise architects must configure compliance logic to enforce the following mandatory parameters: * 15% Foreign Proceeds Threshold: Question 7(b) lowers the Q7(b) reporting threshold to 15%. Any contractor receiving 15% or more of its aggregate revenues, grants, or financial receipts from foreign sources must trigger affirmative disclosure and FOCI analysis. * Expanded Gift and Endowment Scope: Disclosure rules explicitly expand scope to cover foreign gifts, institutional endowments, tuition subsidies, and research grants (specifically impacting academic laboratories and commercial joint ventures). * Foreign Employment Disclosures: Senior management personnel and KMPs holding foreign employment or foreign governance affiliations must execute a mandatory Statement of Full Disclosure of Foreign Affiliations.

Critical Architectural Distinctions: CMMC Floor vs. FCL Eligibility vs. System ATO

A fundamental failure mode in enterprise design is confusing cybersecurity compliance with industrial security authority. The architecture enforces three non-interchangeable tiers: 1. CMMC Maturity Level: Establishes a baseline cybersecurity floor for protecting Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) on non-national security commercial networks. A CMMC assessment does not constitute an FCL and confers no authority to access classified national security information. 2. Commercial Cloud Accreditation Boundaries: Accreditations such as FedRAMP High, AWS GovCloud, or Microsoft Azure Government (GCC High / DoD Impact Levels 4/5) define hosting boundaries and infrastructure security controls only. Commercial cloud hosting does not substitute for NISS facility sponsorship, nor does it yield an FCL. 3. Classified System Authority to Operate (ATO): Operating a contractor information system processing classified material requires executing the Assessment and Authorization (A&A) process under DAAPM v2.2 (historical; now the DAAG) / NIST RMF, leading to a formal ATO issued by the designated DCSA Authorizing Official.

Core AI Boundary Rules

Artificial Intelligence integrated into cleared contractor workflows or DCSA administrative boundaries operates under a strict default-deny posture:

  • Affirmative Assistive Boundary: AI integration is strictly assistive. AI tools are restricted to non-PII administrative triage, educational assistance, CDSE curriculum Q&A, and non-PII facility package checklists.
  • Forbidden Non-Delegable Governance Functions: AI systems cannot perform automated sponsorship intake, replace SEAD 4 adjudicative determinations, execute FOCI evaluations, or render decisions on FOCI mitigation instruments.
  • Hard Data-Handling Default-Deny Rule: SF-86-class Personally Identifiable Information (PII), biometrics (fingerprint streams), and raw FOCI financial/governance artifacts (unredacted cap tables, stock ledgers, foreign revenue receipts, board minutes) are subject to a hard default-deny block. Direct ingesting, prompting, or processing of this data in consumer, commercial, or unapproved AI models is prohibited. All sensitive personnel and FOCI records must route directly into U.S. Government (USG) systems of record (NISS, eApp (DISS), Personnel System of Record).

2.3 Control Framework and Governance

AI interactions within cleared contractor enclaves are governed by four mandatory compliance controls:

  • DLP Default-Deny Signatures: Network and endpoint Data Loss Prevention mechanisms must maintain default-deny rules configured for Social Security Numbers, SF-86 form fields, passport details, foreign financial transaction logs, and cap-table data formats to prevent unauthorized LLM ingestion.
  • Enforceable Corporate AI Policies: Cleared contractors must implement corporate governance directives limiting AI utilization exclusively to non-PII document triage, training assistance, and administrative drafting.
  • Gov-Cloud / Air-Gapped Hosting Preferences: Assistive AI models processing corporate metadata must run within air-gapped enclaves or accredited Government Cloud (Gov-Cloud) environments meeting federal hosting standards.
  • Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Oversight: No AI-generated output may be submitted to DCSA, incorporated into an official facility package, or used to alter an information system security posture without documented review and signature by an FSO, ISSM, or corporate officer.

DCSA Enterprise Vetting and Adjudication Entities

The DCSA enterprise stack integrates three key specialized operational entities responsible for intake risk processing, insider threat analysis, and SEAD 4 adjudications: * Vetting Risk Operations Center (VROC): Conducts continuous vetting (CV) risk assessments, ingests automated data feeds, and manages vetting intake across the cleared workforce. * DoD Insider Threat Management and Analysis Center (DIPMAC): Serves as the enterprise center for ingesting, analyzing, and mitigating insider threat signals and adverse information reports across DoD components and cleared industry. * DoD Consolidated Adjudications Facility (DoD CAF): Renders authoritative personnel security eligibility determinations under SEAD 4 adjudicative guidelines across all investigation tiers.

Personnel Background Investigation Scopes and Benchmarks

Personnel security background investigations managed across the DCSA stack are categorized into two primary tiers with distinct processing benchmarks: * Tier 3 Investigation Scope: Applies to Secret and Confidential eligibility requests. Focuses on a 5-year to 7-year scope covering law enforcement, employment, and financial checks. Primary source benchmark average processing window: ~56 days. * Tier 5 Investigation Scope: Applies to Top Secret, SCI, and SAP eligibility requests. Involves an expanded 10-year scope including in-person field interviews, neighborhood checks, and complex financial audits. Primary source benchmark average processing window: ~80 days.

FOCI Non-Linear Branch Logic and Mitigation Instruments

When foreign ownership, control, or influence is identified during SF 328 evaluation (such as exceeding the May 2025 SF 328 Question 7(b) 15% Q7(b) reporting threshold, expanded foreign gifts/endowments, or foreign management employment), FOCI processing branches into a complex non-linear path. This branching path routinely adds months to the FCL timeline while formal mitigation instruments are negotiated, executed, and audited:

  • Board Resolutions: Used when a foreign interest does not possess sufficient voting stock to elect a director, formally excluding foreign owners from classified access and board deliberations.
  • Security Control Agreements (SCAs): Applied when the company is not effectively owned or controlled by a foreign interest, but foreign representation exists on the board.
  • Special Security Agreements (SSAs): Implemented when a company is effectively owned or controlled by a foreign entity, imposing strict operational isolation and USG oversight access controls.
  • Voting Trusts and Proxy Agreements: The most restrictive instruments, vesting voting rights of foreign-owned stock into vetted US citizen trustees or proxy holders, completely insulating the company's management from foreign influence.
