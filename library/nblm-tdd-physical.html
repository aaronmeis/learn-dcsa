Physical Architecture View of DCSA (Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency)

1. Executive Physical Architecture Overview & System Boundary

This document establishes the authoritative Physical Architecture View for the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) operating model and security program. DCSA administers industrial security for cleared contractor entities under the National Industrial Security Program (NISP) pursuant to 32 CFR Part 117 (National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual—NISPOM).

1. Core Operating Domains

The DCSA physical architecture spans four interlocking defense security domains: 1. Industrial Security / NISP: Facility Security Clearance (FCL) eligibility determination, sponsorship intake, facility package assembly, and Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) evaluation and mitigation. 2. Personnel Vetting: Individual background investigations across Tier 3 (Secret) and Tier 5 (Top Secret) baselines, Personnel Security Clearances (PCL), Security Executive Agent Directive 4 (SEAD 4) adjudications, and Continuous Vetting (CV). 3. Information System (IS) Authorization: Security authorization and Risk Management Framework (RMF) accreditation for contractor classified systems under the DCSA Assessment and Authorization Process Manual (DAAPM v2.2). 4. Counterintelligence (CI): Insider threat mitigation, threat reporting, and foreign intelligence entity (FIE) supply chain risk management across cleared defense facilities.

Statutory Boundary & Eligibility Framework

Per 32 CFR 117.9, facility clearance eligibility requires a verified legal entity structure and legitimate contractual need. An entity must satisfy eleven core statutory criteria prior to FCL issuance: * Legitimate requirement for access to classified information sponsored by a Government Contracting Activity (GCA) or cleared prime contractor. * Formal organization established under the laws of the United States, a U.S. state, or a U.S. territory. * Execution of a Department of Defense Security Agreement (DD Form 441). * Maintenance of a record of business integrity, lawful conduct, and financial viability. * Verification that the entity is not under unmitigated Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI). * Designation and personal clearance of Key Management Personnel (KMP) possessing security-affecting authority. * Appointment of a cleared Facility Security Officer (FSO). * Implementation of physical or procedural controls appropriate to the facility classification (Possessing vs. Non-Possessing). * Absence of conditions posing an unacceptable risk to U.S. national security interests. * Compliance with adverse information reporting and Continuous Vetting protocols. * Operational readiness to undergo recurring Industrial Security Representative (ISR) oversight and annual self-inspections.

Physical Platform Boundary Breakdown

The physical deployment model strictly separates Core U.S. Government (USG) Systems of Record from Cleared Contractor On-Premises Infrastructure:

  • Core USG Systems Boundary (Government Cloud & Hosting Planes):
  • National Industrial Security System (NISS): The primary USG system of record for facility clearances, sponsorship workflows, SF 328 filings, KMP listings, and ISR oversight reporting.
  • National Background Investigation Services (NBIS) / e-QIP: The federal vetting enterprise platform for background investigation initiation, electronic questionnaire ingest (SF-86 family), and biometric fingerprint submission routing.
  • Personnel Eligibility System of Record (historically DISS [verify]): The central DoD repository maintaining real-time personnel eligibility, SEAD 4 adjudicative decisions, incident flags, and Continuous Vetting signals.
  • STEPP / CDSE Portal: The System Administrator Training, Education, and Performance Support platform hosted by the Center for Development of Security Excellence (cdse.edu) providing training transcripts and certification tracking.
  • Enterprise Mission Assurance Support Service (eMASS): The DoD workflow platform tracking RMF Assessment and Authorization (A&A) packages, control implementations, and System Authorizations to Operate (ATO) for contractor classified IT enclaves.
  • Cleared Contractor Estate Boundary (On-Premises & Enterprise Assets):
  • FSO Workstations & Secure Vaults: Endpoint hardware deployed within contractor sites running TLS-encrypted web browser connections to NISS, NBIS, and STEPP, paired with encrypted local repositories for company legal records, bylaws, and stock ledgers.
  • Biometric Capture Infrastructure: USB live-scan fingerprint collection hardware or commercial scheduling endpoints interfacing with accredited fingerprint channelers to transmit digital biometric payloads to NBIS.
  • HRIS-to-FSO Adverse Info Channels: Audited internal reporting channels connecting Human Resource Information Systems (HRIS) directly to the FSO for logging mandatory SEAD 4 reportable events.
  • Local Classified Enclaves & Safes: GSA-approved security containers, closed processing areas, intrusion detection systems (IDS), and isolated local networks deployed within Possessing FCL facilities.

3. Core Physical Systems of Record & On-Premises Topology

3.1 Primary Government Systems of Record

The physical processing of facility and personnel eligibility depends on five core government platforms. Overall end-to-end facility clearance processing averages approximately 180 days from sponsorship acceptance to FCL issuance, assuming standard package completeness and minimal FOCI complexity.

1. National Industrial Security System (NISS)

  • Operational Role: Primary system of record for facility clearances, sponsorship intake, SF 328 filings, KMP listings, self-inspection submissions, and Industrial Security Representative (ISR) oversight logs.
  • Hosting Environment: Dedicated, DCSA-managed secure government cloud environment.
  • Interface Mechanisms: Web-based portal authenticated via USG-issued industry credentials. Serves as the central repository where Day-20 facility packages and SF 328 foreign interest disclosures are uploaded and evaluated.

2. National Background Investigation Services (NBIS) / e-QIP

  • Operational Role: Initiates personnel background investigations, hosts electronic questionnaires (SF-86), and ingests digitized biometric fingerprint payloads for Tier 3 (Secret) and Tier 5 (Top Secret) investigations.
  • Hosting Environment: DoD Vetting Enterprise infrastructure.
  • Interface Mechanisms: Subject and FSO web applications combined with electronic data ingest pipelines from accredited commercial fingerprint channelers.
  • Empirical Investigation Metrics: Background investigation execution timeline averages ~56 days for Tier 3 (Secret) investigations and ~80 days for Tier 5 (Top Secret) investigations.

3. Personnel Eligibility System of Record (DISS [verify])

  • Operational Role: Maintains real-time records of personnel clearance eligibility levels, investigation statuses, SEAD 4 adjudicative decisions, subject transfers, and continuous vetting adverse information flags.
  • Hosting Environment: Centralized DoD personnel security repository ([verify] current exact stack name following legacy JPASS sunset).
  • Interface Mechanisms: Industry security management web portal providing role-based access control (RBAC) to designated company security officials.

4. STEPP (System Administrator Training, Education, and Performance Support)

  • Operational Role: Manages course delivery, exam administration, and compliance transcripts for FSOs, ISSMs, and cleared employees.
  • Hosting Environment: CDSE web infrastructure hosted at cdse.edu.
  • Interface Mechanisms: Direct LMS web application providing automated transcript exports for annual ISR compliance audits. Key curricula include IS140 (Facility Clearances in the NISP), IS142 (Personnel Clearances in the NISP), IS020.CU (Non-Possessing FSO Curriculum), and CS250 (Applying A&A in the NISP).

5. Enterprise Mission Assurance Support Service (eMASS)

  • Operational Role: Government workflow engine for Risk Management Framework (RMF) Assessment and Authorization (A&A) packages supporting contractor classified systems evaluated under DAAPM v2.2.
  • Hosting Environment: Dedicated DoD / DCSA security authorization cloud instance.
  • Interface Mechanisms: ISSM and Authorizing Official (AO) workspaces tracking system boundaries, control implementation summaries (CIS), hardware/software baselines, and Authorization to Operate (ATO) determinations.

3.2 Facility Deployment Models: Possessing vs. Non-Possessing FCL Baseline

NISP contractor facilities are categorized into two physical deployment models based on whether classified materials are physically stored or processed on site.

On-Premises Infrastructure: Standard commercial office space; physical holdings on site are strictly restricted to unclassified corporate and administrative records.

3.3 On-Premises Connectors, Local Infrastructure, and Interfaces

Cleared contractor facilities must deploy specific local hardware, software, and procedural interfaces to connect company operational workflows with USG systems of record.

1. Local Facility Infrastructure Components

  • FSO Workstations & Secure Vaults:
  • Dedicated, encrypted endpoints configured for FSOs to securely access NISS, NBIS, and personnel eligibility systems via TLS.
  • Local company document vaults (physical and digital) storing corporate formation agreements, articles of incorporation, bylaws, key management rosters, ownership charts, and annual self-inspection records.
  • Biometric Fingerprint Collection Hardware:
  • USB live-scan fingerprint scanners or local scheduling terminals used to collect KMP and candidate biometrics during the Day-45 investigation initiation window.
  • Direct TLS encrypted interface to accredited commercial fingerprint channelers for payload transmission into NBIS.
  • HRIS-to-FSO Adverse Info Handoff Channels:
  • Audited internal procedural or software integration connecting corporate Human Resource Information Systems (HRIS) to the FSO.
  • Enforces a hard service level agreement (SLA) ensuring mandatory SEAD 4 reportable events (e.g., foreign travel, personal status changes, disciplinary actions, arrests) are transmitted to the FSO without stalling in HR.
  • Corporate Legal & Cap Table Vaults:
  • Local, highly restricted corporate databases holding capitalization tables, stock ledgers, board minutes, and foreign proceeds ledgers.
  • Provides verified financial data to populate the May 2025 revised SF 328 and maintain continuous compliance with the 15% foreign proceeds threshold.

5.2 Enterprise Cloud Disambiguation Rules

  1. CMMC establishes a CUI cybersecurity floor, not a Facility Security Clearance (FCL): Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) validates unclassified CUI safeguarding across contractor networks. It does not fulfill NISP requirements, substitute for an SF 328 FOCI evaluation, or grant facility security eligibility.
  2. GCC High / FedRAMP authorization provides cloud hosting support, not NISS sponsorship or FCL status: Acquiring a commercial FedRAMP High or Government Community Cloud (GCC/GCCH) tenant does not grant an FCL. A contractor cannot self-sponsor or claim cleared facility status based solely on cloud hosting infrastructure.
  3. Classified system processing on DoD IL / Secret clouds requires DAAPM + NIST RMF system ATO: Processing classified data within high-impact cloud enclaves (e.g., DoD IL5/IL6) requires an explicit System Authorization to Operate (ATO) generated under DAAPM v2.2 and NIST RMF guidelines. Commercial FedRAMP High or GCC High authorizations do not satisfy classified ATO requirements.
  4. Self-sponsorship into the NISP is strictly prohibited regardless of cloud tenant tier: Facility clearance processing requires legitimate sponsorship initiated in NISS by a Government Contracting Activity (GCA) or a cleared prime contractor based on an active contractual need (DD Form 254). Tenant tier or cloud accreditation level never bypasses this requirement.

6. Physical Baseline Revision: May 2025 SF 328 & NISS Deployment

6.1 Key Change Parameters

On 1 May 2025, the USG formally approved an updated Certificate Pertaining to Foreign Interests (Form SF 328). This revised physical baseline was deployed live into NISS on 12 May 2025.

Summary of Physical Revision Parameters:

  • Form Approval & Deployment Timelines:
  • Form Approval Date: 1 May 2025.
  • NISS System Deployment Date: 12 May 2025.
  • Question 7(b) Threshold Reduction:
  • The aggregate foreign proceeds reporting threshold in Question 7(b) was lowered from 30% down to 15%.
  • Forces cleared contractors to track aggregate foreign financial flows at a significantly lower sensitivity floor.
  • Expanded Disclosure Scope:
  • Mandatory reporting expanded beyond traditional operational revenue to include foreign gifts, tuition assistance, and institutional endowments.
  • Directly impacts academic institutions, research laboratories, and commercial entities receiving foreign grant funding.
  • Foreign Employment of Key Leadership:
  • Mandates the formal execution of a Statement of Full Disclosure of Foreign Affiliations whenever foreign entities employ or engage company management personnel.
